Printable visual schedules, Now/Next boards, and supports to increase independence

Why Visual Supports?

Visual supports help twice exceptional children understand expectations, transitions, and daily routines. They reduce anxiety by making the abstract concrete and provide a reliable reference that children can access independently.

These tools are particularly helpful for children who are visual learners, have executive function challenges, or experience anxiety around transitions and changes in routine.

Tips for Using Visual Supports

Getting Started

  • Start with one visual support at a time to avoid overwhelming your child
  • Place visuals at your child's eye level in relevant locations
  • Introduce the visual support during a calm time, not in the moment of need
  • Model how to use the visual support yourself

Making Them Effective

  • Laminate visuals for durability and reusability
  • Use velcro to make schedules interactive and adaptable
  • Add your child's favorite characters or interests to increase engagement
  • Refer to visuals consistently - "Let's check the schedule!"
  • Update and adjust visuals as your child's needs change
  • Celebrate when your child uses the visual independently

Common Challenges

  • Child ignores the visual: Try adding their input in creating it, or use more engaging images
  • Too overwhelming: Simplify to fewer steps or images
  • Resists transitions despite visuals: Add a timer or countdown to prepare for changes
  • Works at first then stops: Rotate between different visual formats to maintain interest
